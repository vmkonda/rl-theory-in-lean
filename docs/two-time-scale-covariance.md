# Asymptotic Covariance of Linear Two-Time-Scale Stochastic Approximation with Markov Noise

*Vijaymohan Konda · November 2026*

*Generated from the LaTeX source in this directory; the PDF has the same numbering.*

> **Abstract.**
>
> For the coupled linear iterations $`x_{k+1} = x_k - \beta_k(A_{11}x_k + A_{12}y_k) + \beta_kV_k`$ and $`y_{k+1} = y_k - \gamma_k(A_{21}x_k + A_{22}y_k) + \gamma_kW_k`$ with $`\beta_k/\gamma_k \to 0`$ and noise generated by a Markov chain, we prove that $`\beta_k^{-1}\mathbb{E}[x_kx_k^\top]`$ converges to the solution of the Lyapunov equation of the single-time-scale iteration $`x_{k+1} = x_k - \beta_k\Delta x_k + \beta_k(V_k - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}W_k)`$, with $`\Delta = A_{11} - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}A_{21}`$. This extends Theorems 7.6 and 7.8 of Konda (2002), proved there for i.i.d. noise, to Markov noise. As in the single-time-scale note, the proof reduces Markov noise to martingale differences with the Poisson equation and then studies deterministic recursions for the second moments; the new ingredient is a lemma on three coupled recursions running on two time scales. This is step 2 of the asymptotic covariance analysis of actor-critic algorithms.

## 1 Setting and assumptions

Let $`(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_k)_{k\ge0}, \mathbb P)`$ be a filtered probability space and consider, for $`x_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_1}`$ and $`y_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_2}`$,
``` math
\begin{aligned}
  x_{k+1} &= x_k - \beta_k(A_{11}x_k + A_{12}y_k) + \beta_kV_k,\\
  y_{k+1} &= y_k - \gamma_k(A_{21}x_k + A_{22}y_k) + \gamma_kW_k,
\end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
```
with $`(x_0, y_0)`$ $`\mathcal{F}_0`$-measurable and square integrable and $`(V_k, W_k)`$ $`\mathcal{F}_{k+1}`$-measurable. When $`A_{22}`$ is invertible, let
``` math
D = A_{22}^{-1}A_{21},\qquad \Delta = A_{11} - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}A_{21} = A_{11} - A_{12}D .
```
The fast iterate $`y_k`$ tracks $`-Dx_k`$, the solution of $`A_{21}x + A_{22}y = 0`$, and with $`y = -Dx`$ the slow iteration becomes $`x_{k+1} = x_k - \beta_k\Delta x_k + \beta_kV_k`$. Norms of matrices are operator norms.

<div id="ass:step" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1.1** (Step sizes). $`\beta_k, \gamma_k > 0`$ are deterministic and tend to zero; $`\beta_{k+1}^{-1} - \beta_k^{-1} \to \bar\beta \ge 0`$ and $`\gamma_{k+1}^{-1} - \gamma_k^{-1} \to 0`$; and
``` math
\frac{\beta_k}{\gamma_k} \to 0,\qquad \frac{\gamma_k^2}{\beta_k} \to 0 .
```

</div>

For example $`\beta_k = \alpha/(k + k_0)`$ and $`\gamma_k = c/(k + k_0)^\rho`$ with $`\frac12 < \rho < 1`$, which gives $`\bar\beta = 1/\alpha`$.

<div id="ass:stable" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1.2** (Stability). The eigenvalues of $`A_{22}`$ and of $`\Delta_{\bar\beta} := \Delta - \frac{\bar\beta}{2}I`$ have positive real parts.

</div>

<div id="ass:noise" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1.3** (Noise). The noise decomposes as
``` math
V_k = M_{k+1} + h_k - h_{k+1},\qquad W_k = M'_{k+1} + g_k - g_{k+1},
```
where $`h_k, g_k`$ are $`\mathcal{F}_k`$-measurable, $`M_{k+1}, M'_{k+1}`$ are $`\mathcal{F}_{k+1}`$-measurable with $`\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = \mathbb{E}[M'_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = 0`$, all four have bounded second moments, and
``` math
\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M_{k+1}^\top] \to \Gamma_{11},\qquad
  \mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M'^\top_{k+1}] \to \Gamma_{12} = \Gamma_{21}^\top,\qquad
  \mathbb{E}[M'_{k+1}M'^\top_{k+1}] \to \Gamma_{22}.
```

</div>

By Section 4 of the single-time-scale note, Assumption [1.3](#ass:noise) holds when $`(V_k, W_k)`$ is a function of a finite Markov chain or of a chain with eligibility traces, and then $`\Gamma = \begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{11} & \Gamma_{12}\\ \Gamma_{21} & \Gamma_{22}\end{pmatrix}`$ is the long-run covariance of $`(V_k, W_k)`$.

<div id="rem:gamma2" class="remark">

**Remark 1.4** (The condition $`\gamma_k^2 = o(\beta_k)`$). With martingale-difference noise ($`h = g = 0`$) this condition is not needed. With Markov noise the telescoping part of $`W_k`$ shifts the fast iterate by $`\gamma_kg_k`$, and we bound its correlation with the slow iterate, of size $`\sqrt{\beta_k}`$, by Cauchy–Schwarz; the condition makes that bound $`o(\beta_k)`$. It holds for the usual schedules above.

</div>

## 2 Main result

Let $`\hat y_k = y_k + Dx_k`$ be the deviation of the fast iterate from its quasi-static value.

<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 2.1**. Under Assumptions [1.1](#ass:step)–[1.3](#ass:noise),
``` math
\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_kx_k^\top]}{\beta_k} \to \Sigma_{11},\qquad
  \frac{\mathbb{E}[x_k\hat y_k^\top]}{\beta_k} \to \Sigma_{12},\qquad
  \frac{\mathbb{E}[\hat y_k\hat y_k^\top]}{\gamma_k} \to \Sigma_{22},
```
where $`\Sigma_{22}`$, $`\Sigma_{12}`$, $`\Sigma_{11}`$ are the unique solutions of
``` math
\begin{align}
  A_{22}\Sigma_{22} + \Sigma_{22}A_{22}^\top &= \Gamma_{22}, \label{eq:s22}\\
  \Sigma_{12}A_{22}^\top + A_{12}\Sigma_{22} &= \Gamma_{12}, \label{eq:s12}\\
  \Delta_{\bar\beta}\Sigma_{11} + \Sigma_{11}\Delta_{\bar\beta}^\top + A_{12}\Sigma_{12}^\top + \Sigma_{12}A_{12}^\top
    &= \Gamma_{11}. \label{eq:s11}
\end{align}
```

</div>

*Lean:* `tendsto_two_time_scale_covariance`, fully formalized. There $`D`$ is any matrix with $`A_{22}D = A_{21}`$, stability takes the positive-definite form of Remark 1.3 of the single-time-scale note for $`A_{22}`$ and $`\Delta_{\bar\beta}`$, and the noise hypotheses are as in Theorem 2.1 of that note.

These are equations (7.14)–(7.16) of Konda (2002), there for i.i.d. noise. Eliminating $`\Sigma_{12}`$ and $`\Sigma_{22}`$ gives the analogue of Theorem 7.8 there.

<div id="cor:eff" class="corollary">

**Corollary 2.2**. $`\Sigma_{11}`$ is the unique solution of
``` math
\Delta_{\bar\beta}\Sigma_{11} + \Sigma_{11}\Delta_{\bar\beta}^\top = \tilde\Gamma,\qquad
  \tilde\Gamma = \Gamma_{11} - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}\Gamma_{21} - \Gamma_{12}A_{22}^{-\top}A_{12}^\top
    + A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}\Gamma_{22}A_{22}^{-\top}A_{12}^\top .
```
$`\tilde\Gamma`$ is the long-run covariance of the effective noise $`V_k - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}W_k`$, so the slow iterate has the asymptotic covariance of the single-time-scale iteration $`x_{k+1} = x_k - \beta_k\Delta x_k + \beta_k(V_k - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}W_k)`$ (Theorem 2.1 of the single-time-scale note).

</div>

<div class="remark">

**Remark 2.3** (Numerical check). For a 6-state chain with $`d_1 = d_2 = 1`$, $`A = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0.8\\ -0.5 & 1.5\end{pmatrix}`$, $`\beta_k = \alpha/(k + 50)`$ with $`\alpha = 2/\Delta`$ and $`\gamma_k = (k + 50)^{-0.7}`$, a simulation with $`20{,}000`$ runs gives $`\beta_k^{-1}\mathbb{E}[x_k^2] \approx 1.131`$ at $`k = 10^5`$, against $`\Sigma_{11} = 1.142`$; the noise $`V`$ alone would give $`0.606`$ (`experiments/check_two_time_scale.py`).

</div>

## 3 Proof

Throughout, $`C`$ denotes a constant independent of $`k`$, and $`\epsilon_k`$ a sequence tending to zero; both may change from line to line. By Assumption [1.1](#ass:step), $`\beta_k\le\gamma_k`$ for large $`k`$; we assume this for all $`k`$, which changes nothing.

### 3.1 Change of variables

<div id="lem:cov" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3.1**. Let $`\tilde x_k = x_k + \beta_kh_k`$ and $`\tilde y_k = \hat y_k + \gamma_kg_k + \beta_kDh_k`$. Then
``` math
\begin{align*}
  \tilde x_{k+1} &= \tilde x_k - \beta_k(\Delta\tilde x_k + A_{12}\tilde y_k) + \beta_kM_{k+1} + c_k,\\
  \tilde y_{k+1} &= \tilde y_k - \gamma_kA_{22}\tilde y_k - \beta_kD(\Delta\tilde x_k + A_{12}\tilde y_k)
    + \gamma_kM'_{k+1} + \beta_kDM_{k+1} + c'_k,
\end{align*}
```
with $`\mathbb{E}\|c_k\|^2 \le C\beta_k^2\gamma_k^2`$ and $`\mathbb{E}\|c'_k\|^2 \le C\gamma_k^4`$.

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Since $`A_{21}x + A_{22}y = A_{22}\hat y`$ and $`A_{11}x + A_{12}y = \Delta x + A_{12}\hat y`$,
``` math
\begin{align*}
  x_{k+1} &= x_k - \beta_k(\Delta x_k + A_{12}\hat y_k) + \beta_kV_k,\\
  \hat y_{k+1} &= \hat y_k - \gamma_kA_{22}\hat y_k - \beta_kD(\Delta x_k + A_{12}\hat y_k) + \gamma_kW_k + \beta_kDV_k .
\end{align*}
```
Now substitute the decompositions of $`V_k`$ and $`W_k`$ and proceed as in Lemma 2.3 of the single-time-scale note. One finds
``` math
\begin{align*}
  c_k &= \beta_k\big(\beta_k\Delta h_k + \gamma_kA_{12}g_k + \beta_kA_{12}Dh_k\big) + (\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k)h_{k+1},\\
  c'_k &= \gamma_kA_{22}(\gamma_kg_k + \beta_kDh_k) + \beta_kD\big(\beta_k\Delta h_k + \gamma_kA_{12}g_k + \beta_kA_{12}Dh_k\big)\\
    &\quad + (\gamma_{k+1} - \gamma_k)g_{k+1} + (\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k)Dh_{k+1},
\end{align*}
```
and the bounds follow from $`|\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k| \le C\beta_k^2`$, $`|\gamma_{k+1} - \gamma_k| \le C\gamma_k^2`$ and $`\beta_k \le \gamma_k`$. ◻

</div>

*Lean:* `ttsX_succ`, `ttsY_succ` (the identities), `norm_tts_cx_le`, `norm_tts_cy_le` (pointwise bounds on $`c_k`$, $`c'_k`$).

### 3.2 Second-moment recursions

Let $`X_k = \mathbb{E}[\tilde x_k\tilde x_k^\top]`$, $`Z_k = \mathbb{E}[\tilde x_k\tilde y_k^\top]`$ and $`Y_k = \mathbb{E}[\tilde y_k\tilde y_k^\top]`$, and
``` math
n_k = 1 + \frac{\|X_k\|}{\beta_k} + \frac{\|Z_k\|}{\beta_k} + \frac{\|Y_k\|}{\gamma_k}.
```

<div id="lem:rec" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3.2**.
``` math
\begin{align*}
  X_{k+1} &= X_k - \beta_k\big(\Delta X_k + X_k\Delta^\top + A_{12}Z_k^\top + Z_kA_{12}^\top\big)
    + \beta_k^2\Gamma_{11} + R_k, & \|R_k\| &\le \epsilon_k\beta_k^2n_k,\\
  Z_{k+1} &= Z_k - \gamma_kZ_kA_{22}^\top - \beta_kA_{12}Y_k + \beta_k\gamma_k\Gamma_{12} + R'_k,
    & \|R'_k\| &\le \epsilon_k\beta_k\gamma_kn_k,\\
  Y_{k+1} &= Y_k - \gamma_k\big(A_{22}Y_k + Y_kA_{22}^\top\big) + \gamma_k^2\Gamma_{22} + R''_k,
    & \|R''_k\| &\le \epsilon_k\gamma_k^2n_k .
\end{align*}
```

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Write $`\tilde x_{k+1} = a + b + c`$ and $`\tilde y_{k+1} = a' + b' + c'`$, with $`a, a'`$ the $`\mathcal{F}_k`$-measurable parts, $`b = \beta_kM_{k+1}`$ and $`b' = \gamma_kM'_{k+1} + \beta_kDM_{k+1}`$ the martingale differences, and $`c = c_k`$, $`c' = c'_k`$. For any two of these sums,
``` math
\mathbb{E}[(a + b + c)(a' + b' + c')^\top] = \mathbb{E}[aa'^\top] + \mathbb{E}[bb'^\top] + \rho,
```
where $`\mathbb{E}[ab'^\top] = \mathbb{E}[ba'^\top] = 0`$ by the tower property and $`\rho`$ collects the five terms involving $`c`$ or $`c'`$, each bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz. We use $`\mathbb{E}\|\tilde x_k\|^2 \le d_1\|X_k\| \le C\beta_kn_k`$ and $`\mathbb{E}\|\tilde y_k\|^2 \le C\gamma_kn_k`$.

*The $`Z`$ recursion.* With $`w = \Delta\tilde x_k + A_{12}\tilde y_k`$, $`a = \tilde x_k - \beta_kw`$ and $`a' = \tilde y_k - \gamma_kA_{22}\tilde y_k - \beta_kDw`$, so
``` math
\mathbb{E}[aa'^\top] = Z_k - \gamma_kZ_kA_{22}^\top - \beta_k\mathbb{E}[w\tilde y_k^\top] - \beta_k\mathbb{E}[\tilde x_kw^\top]D^\top
    + \beta_k\gamma_k\mathbb{E}[ww'^\top] + \beta_k^2\mathbb{E}[ww^\top]D^\top
```
with $`w' = A_{22}\tilde y_k`$. Here $`\mathbb{E}[w\tilde y_k^\top] = \Delta Z_k + A_{12}Y_k`$; the term $`\beta_k\Delta Z_k`$ is at most $`\beta_k^2n_k = (\beta_k/\gamma_k)\beta_k\gamma_kn_k`$, and $`\|\mathbb{E}[\tilde x_kw^\top]\| \le C\beta_kn_k`$ in the same way. Further $`\mathbb{E}\|w\|^2 \le C\gamma_kn_k`$, so $`\beta_k\gamma_k\|\mathbb{E}[ww'^\top]\| \le C\beta_k\gamma_k^2n_k`$ and $`\beta_k^2\|\mathbb{E}[ww^\top]\| \le C\beta_k^2\gamma_kn_k`$. The martingale part is $`\mathbb{E}[bb'^\top] = \beta_k\gamma_k\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M'^\top_{k+1}] + \beta_k^2\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M_{k+1}^\top]D^\top`$, and $`\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M'^\top_{k+1}] - \Gamma_{12} \to 0`$. Finally, by Lemma [3.1](#lem:cov),
``` math
\|\rho\| \le C\Big(\sqrt{\beta_kn_k}\,\gamma_k^2 + \beta_k\gamma_k^2 + \beta_k\gamma_k\sqrt{\gamma_kn_k}
    + \beta_k\gamma_k^2 + \beta_k\gamma_k^3\Big)
  \le C\beta_k\gamma_k\Big(\frac{\gamma_k}{\sqrt{\beta_k}} + \sqrt{\gamma_k}\Big)n_k,
```
using $`\sqrt{n} \le n`$; this is where $`\gamma_k^2/\beta_k \to 0`$ is used.

*The $`X`$ and $`Y`$ recursions* are similar and simpler. For $`X`$, the term $`\beta_k^2\mathbb{E}[ww^\top]`$ is at most $`C\beta_k^2\gamma_kn_k`$, and the cross terms with $`c`$ are at most $`C\sqrt{\beta_kn_k}\,\beta_k\gamma_k \le C\beta_k^2(\gamma_k/\sqrt{\beta_k})n_k`$. For $`Y`$, the terms of order $`\beta_k`$ are at most $`C\beta_k\gamma_kn_k = (\beta_k/\gamma_k)\gamma_k^2n_k`$, and the cross terms with $`c'`$ are at most $`C\sqrt{\gamma_kn_k}\,\gamma_k^2`$. ◻

</div>

*Lean:* `tts_remainder_step`, from the one-step expansions `tts_step_X`, `tts_step_Z`, `tts_step_Y` (built on `crossMoment_step`), the size bounds `tts_sizes`, and the arithmetic of `tts_R1_le`, `tts_R2_le`, `tts_R3_le`.

### 3.3 A deterministic lemma for coupled recursions

<div id="lem:coupled" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3.3**. Let $`\mathcal{L}_1, \mathcal{L}_2, \mathcal{L}_3`$ be linear maps on finite-dimensional real inner product spaces $`\mathcal V_1, \mathcal V_2, \mathcal V_3`$ with $`\langle\mathcal{L}_iv, v\rangle \ge \eta\|v\|^2`$ for some $`\eta > 0`$, and let $`K_{12}\colon \mathcal V_2\to\mathcal V_1`$ and $`K_{23}\colon\mathcal V_3\to\mathcal V_2`$ be linear. Let $`\beta_k, \gamma_k \to 0`$ be positive with $`\sum_k\beta_k = \infty`$ and $`\beta_k \le \gamma_k`$. Suppose
``` math
\begin{align*}
  a_{k+1} &= a_k - \beta_k(\mathcal{L}_1a_k + K_{12}c_k - g_1) + \beta_k\delta^1_k,\\
  c_{k+1} &= c_k - \gamma_k(\mathcal{L}_2c_k + K_{23}b_k - g_2) + \gamma_k\delta^2_k,\\
  b_{k+1} &= b_k - \gamma_k(\mathcal{L}_3b_k - g_3) + \gamma_k\delta^3_k,
\end{align*}
```
with $`\|\delta^i_k\| \le \epsilon_k(1 + \|a_k\| + \|b_k\| + \|c_k\|)`$ and $`\epsilon_k \to 0`$. Then $`b_k \to b^*`$, $`c_k \to c^*`$ and $`a_k \to a^*`$, the unique solutions of $`\mathcal{L}_3b^* = g_3`$, $`\mathcal{L}_2c^* + K_{23}b^* = g_2`$ and $`\mathcal{L}_1a^* + K_{12}c^* = g_1`$.

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The solutions exist and are unique because each $`\mathcal{L}_i`$ is coercive, hence invertible. Let $`e^a = a - a^*`$, $`e^c = c - c^*`$, $`e^b = b - b^*`$. Then
``` math
\begin{align*}
  e^a_{k+1} &= (I - \beta_k\mathcal{L}_1)e^a_k + \beta_k(\delta^1_k - K_{12}e^c_k),\\
  e^c_{k+1} &= (I - \gamma_k\mathcal{L}_2)e^c_k + \gamma_k(\delta^2_k - K_{23}e^b_k),\\
  e^b_{k+1} &= (I - \gamma_k\mathcal{L}_3)e^b_k + \gamma_k\delta^3_k ,
\end{align*}
```
and $`\|\delta^i_k\| \le \epsilon_k(S + \|e^a_k\| + \|e^b_k\| + \|e^c_k\|)`$ with $`S = 1 + \|a^*\| + \|b^*\| + \|c^*\|`$. For small $`t`$, $`\|(I - t\mathcal{L}_i)v\| \le (1 - \eta t/2)\|v\|`$.

*Boundedness.* Let $`\kappa = \eta/2`$, $`K_b = 1/\kappa`$, $`K_c = (\|K_{23}\|K_b + 1)/\kappa`$, $`K_a = (\|K_{12}\|K_c + 1)/\kappa`$ and $`s = S + K_a + K_b + K_c`$. Take $`k_0`$ such that for $`k \ge k_0`$ the contraction bounds hold and $`\epsilon_ks \le 1`$, and take $`\lambda \ge 1`$ with $`\|e^a_{k_0}\| \le \lambda K_a`$, $`\|e^b_{k_0}\| \le \lambda K_b`$, $`\|e^c_{k_0}\| \le \lambda K_c`$. If these three bounds hold at $`k \ge k_0`$, then $`\|\delta^i_k\| \le \epsilon_k\lambda s \le \lambda`$, and
``` math
\begin{align*}
  \|e^b_{k+1}\| &\le (1 - \kappa\gamma_k)\lambda K_b + \gamma_k\lambda = \lambda K_b,\\
  \|e^c_{k+1}\| &\le (1 - \kappa\gamma_k)\lambda K_c + \gamma_k(\lambda + \|K_{23}\|\lambda K_b) = \lambda K_c,
\end{align*}
```
and similarly $`\|e^a_{k+1}\| \le \lambda K_a`$. So $`a_k, b_k, c_k`$ are bounded and $`\delta^i_k \to 0`$.

*Convergence.* Lemma 2.7 of the single-time-scale note (with step sizes $`\gamma_k`$, $`\sum\gamma_k \ge \sum\beta_k = \infty`$) gives $`e^b_k \to 0`$; then the perturbation $`\delta^2_k - K_{23}e^b_k \to 0`$ gives $`e^c_k \to 0`$; then $`e^a_k \to 0`$ in the same way. ◻

</div>

*Lean:* `tendsto_zero_of_coupled_recursion`; the normalization of the next subsection is `tendsto_two_time_scale_moments`.

### 3.4 Proof of Theorem [2.1](#thm:main)

Let $`a_k = X_k/\beta_k`$, $`c_k = Z_k/\beta_k`$, $`b_k = Y_k/\gamma_k`$, so $`n_k = 1 + \|a_k\| + \|b_k\| + \|c_k\|`$. Write $`\beta_k/\beta_{k+1} = 1 + \beta_k(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)`$ and $`\gamma_k/\gamma_{k+1} = 1 + \gamma_k\epsilon'_k`$ with $`\epsilon_k, \epsilon'_k \to 0`$. Dividing the recursions of Lemma [3.2](#lem:rec) by $`\beta_{k+1}`$, $`\beta_{k+1}`$ and $`\gamma_{k+1}`$:
``` math
\begin{align*}
  a_{k+1} &= a_k - \beta_k\big(\Delta_{\bar\beta}a_k + a_k\Delta_{\bar\beta}^\top + A_{12}c_k^\top + c_kA_{12}^\top - \Gamma_{11}\big)
    + \beta_k\delta^1_k,\\
  c_{k+1} &= c_k - \gamma_k\big(c_kA_{22}^\top + A_{12}b_k - \Gamma_{12}\big) + \gamma_k\delta^2_k,\\
  b_{k+1} &= b_k - \gamma_k\big(A_{22}b_k + b_kA_{22}^\top - \Gamma_{22}\big) + \gamma_k\delta^3_k ,
\end{align*}
```
where, for instance, $`\delta^2_k = \frac{\beta_k}{\beta_{k+1}}\frac{R'_k}{\beta_k\gamma_k} + \frac{\beta_k}{\gamma_k}(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)
\big(c_k - \gamma_k(c_kA_{22}^\top + A_{12}b_k - \Gamma_{12})\big)`$. The factors $`\beta_k/\gamma_k`$, $`\gamma_k`$, $`\epsilon_k`$ and $`\epsilon'_k`$ all tend to zero, so $`\|\delta^i_k\| \le \epsilon_k n_k`$. The three maps $`\mathcal{L}_1a = \Delta_{\bar\beta}a + a\Delta_{\bar\beta}^\top`$, $`\mathcal{L}_2c = cA_{22}^\top`$ and $`\mathcal{L}_3b = A_{22}b + bA_{22}^\top`$ are coercive in the inner products of Remark 1.3 of the single-time-scale note, so Lemma [3.3](#lem:coupled) applies with $`K_{12}c = A_{12}c^\top + cA_{12}^\top`$ and $`K_{23}b = A_{12}b`$, and gives the three limits for the shifted iterates. Finally $`x_k = \tilde x_k - \beta_kh_k`$ and $`\hat y_k = \tilde y_k - \gamma_kg_k - \beta_kDh_k`$, and as at the end of the proof of Theorem 2.1 of the single-time-scale note the differences are of lower order.  ◻

### 3.5 Proof of Corollary [2.2](#cor:eff)

By [(??eq:s12)](#eq:s12), $`\Sigma_{12} = (\Gamma_{12} - A_{12}\Sigma_{22})A_{22}^{-\top}`$, so
``` math
A_{12}\Sigma_{12}^\top + \Sigma_{12}A_{12}^\top
  = A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}\Gamma_{21} + \Gamma_{12}A_{22}^{-\top}A_{12}^\top
    - A_{12}\big(A_{22}^{-1}\Sigma_{22} + \Sigma_{22}A_{22}^{-\top}\big)A_{12}^\top,
```
and by [(??eq:s22)](#eq:s22), $`A_{22}^{-1}\Sigma_{22} + \Sigma_{22}A_{22}^{-\top}
= A_{22}^{-1}(\Sigma_{22}A_{22}^\top + A_{22}\Sigma_{22})A_{22}^{-\top} = A_{22}^{-1}\Gamma_{22}A_{22}^{-\top}`$. Substituting in [(??eq:s11)](#eq:s11) gives the equation for $`\Sigma_{11}`$. The effective noise $`V_k - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}W_k`$ has the Poisson decomposition with martingale part $`M_{k+1} - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}M'_{k+1}`$, whose covariance tends to $`\tilde\Gamma`$.  ◻

*Lean:* `effective_lyapunov` (the algebra, using that $`\Sigma_{22}`$ is symmetric, `transCLM_eq_of_lyapOp`) and `tendsto_two_time_scale_slow` (the corollary as a limit statement, with $`A_{22}^{-1}`$ given as a matrix $`G`$ with $`GA_{22} = I`$).

## 4 Multiplicative noise

In actor-critic algorithms all four blocks of the matrix are random. Consider
``` math
\begin{aligned}
  x_{k+1} &= x_k - \beta_k\big((A_{11} + \tilde A^{11}_k)x_k + (A_{12} + \tilde A^{12}_k)y_k\big) + \beta_kV_k,\\
  y_{k+1} &= y_k - \gamma_k\big((A_{21} + \tilde A^{21}_k)x_k + (A_{22} + \tilde A^{22}_k)y_k\big) + \gamma_kW_k,
\end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
```
with $`V_k, W_k`$ as in Assumption [1.3](#ass:noise) and $`\tilde A^{ij}_k = N^{ij}_{k+1} + G^{ij}_k - G^{ij}_{k+1}`$, where $`G^{ij}_k`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_k`$-measurable, $`N^{ij}_{k+1}`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_{k+1}`$-measurable with $`\mathbb{E}[N^{ij}_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = 0`$ entrywise, and all are bounded by a constant almost surely (as in Section 3 of the single-time-scale note).

<div id="thm:mult" class="theorem">

**Theorem 4.1**. *Under Assumptions [1.1](#ass:step)–[1.3](#ass:noise) and the above conditions on the matrix noise, the conclusions of Theorem [2.1](#thm:main) and Corollary [2.2](#cor:eff) hold for [(2)](#eq:mult), with the same $`\Gamma`$: the covariances of the martingale parts $`M_{k+1}, M'_{k+1}`$ of the additive noise.*

</div>

*Lean:* `tendsto_mtts` and `tendsto_mtts_slow`, fully formalized; the matrix noise is the structure `TTSMatrixNoise`. For additive noise driven by a finite chain and an eligibility trace on both time scales, `tendsto_mtts_slow_trace` discharges the three covariance hypotheses. The setting is that of an actor and its critic, sharing the chain and the critic’s trace. The martingale parts come from the Poisson solutions of Lemma 4.4 of the companion note, and $`\Gamma_{11}`$, $`\Gamma_{12}`$, $`\Gamma_{22}`$ are computed from the stationary first and second moments of the trace (`traceGamma`, `traceCross`, via `tendsto_crossMoment_of_moments`). The matrix noise there is still the abstract bounded structure.

<div class="proof">

*Proof sketch.* As in Section 3 of the single-time-scale note, write $`\tilde A^{11}_kx_k + \tilde A^{12}_ky_k = N^{11}_{k+1}x_k + N^{12}_{k+1}y_k + (G^{11}_kx_k + G^{12}_ky_k)
- (G^{11}_{k+1}x_{k+1} + G^{12}_{k+1}y_{k+1}) + \rho_k`$ with $`\rho_k = G^{11}_{k+1}(x_{k+1} - x_k) + G^{12}_{k+1}(y_{k+1} - y_k)`$, and similarly for the fast equation. Then [(2)](#eq:mult) is the additive iteration [(1)](#eq:iter) with

- telescoping parts $`\eta_k = h_k - G^{11}_kx_k - G^{12}_ky_k`$ and $`\zeta_k = g_k - G^{21}_kx_k - G^{22}_ky_k`$, which now depend on the iterates;

- martingale parts $`m_{k+1} = M_{k+1} - N^{11}_{k+1}x_k - N^{12}_{k+1}y_k`$ and $`m'_{k+1}`$, whose covariances differ from $`\Gamma_k`$ by $`O(R_k + R_k^2)`$ with $`R_k = \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|x_k\|^2} + \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|y_k\|^2}`$;

- extra remainders $`\rho_k, \rho'_k`$ with $`\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\rho_k\|^2} \le C\gamma_k(1 + R_k)`$, because $`y_{k+1} - y_k = O(\gamma_k)`$.

The proof of Theorem [2.1](#thm:main) goes through for such perturbed iterations. All sizes of the martingale parts and of the remainders now grow at most like $`1 + R_k`$, and $`R_k \le C(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\tilde x_k\|^2} + \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\tilde y_k\|^2} + \gamma_k) \le C\sqrt{n_k}`$ once $`\gamma_k`$ is small enough to invert the shift. The bounds of Lemma [3.2](#lem:rec) then hold with $`\sqrt{n_k}`$ factors on every size, which does not change their form, and the covariance errors are $`O\big((\sqrt{\beta_k} + \sqrt{\gamma_k})\,n_k\big) = o(n_k)`$. The finitely many initial steps before $`\gamma_k`$ is small are removed by a shift in time. ◻

</div>

*Lean:* the perturbed form is `tendsto_tts_perturbed`, built on `tts_perturbed_step` and the general one-step bound `tts_remainder_step_gen`; the reduction of [(2)](#eq:mult) to it is `tts_of_mtts`, and the inversion of the shift under small steps is `inv_shift_le`.

## 5 What remains

- *Nonlinear algorithms.* The transfer to the actual two-time-scale iteration is now Section 2 of `actor-critic-rates`: Theorem 2.5 (`tendsto_tts_taylor`), the fourth-moment bounds of Theorems 2.6–2.7 (`moment_bounds_tts`, `moment_bounds_tts_markov`), and $`\theta`$-dependent sampling with the noise covariances derived under a Doeblin kernel (`tendsto_tts_param_chain_doeblin`). Remainders of order $`\gamma_k`$ are allowed in both components, which covers an actor whose noise depends on the critic.

- *Global dissipativity.* Those results assume a globally dissipative drift. A local theory, by localization, is not formalized.
