# Asymptotic Covariance of Linear Stochastic Approximation with Markov Noise

*Vijaymohan Konda · November 2026*

*Generated from the LaTeX source in this directory; the PDF has the same numbering.*

> **Abstract.**
>
> For the linear iteration $`\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k - \beta_kA\theta_k + \beta_kV_k`$ with noise $`V_k`$ generated by a Markov chain, we prove that $`\beta_k^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\theta_k\theta_k^\top]`$ converges to the solution $`\Sigma`$ of a Lyapunov equation whose right-hand side is the long-run covariance $`\Gamma`$ of the noise. The proof reduces Markov noise to martingale-difference noise with the Poisson equation, and then analyses a deterministic recursion for the second moments, as in the i.i.d. case. Only two lemmas involve probability; the rest is matrix analysis. We verify the hypotheses for noise driven by a finite chain and for noise driven by eligibility traces. This is the first of four steps towards the asymptotic covariance of actor-critic algorithms: single time scale with additive noise (this note), two time scales, multiplicative noise, and the transfer to the nonlinear algorithm.

## 1 Setting and assumptions

Let $`(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_k)_{k\ge0}, \mathbb P)`$ be a filtered probability space, $`A \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}`$, and consider
``` math
  \theta_{k+1} = \theta_k - \beta_kA\theta_k + \beta_kV_k, \qquad k \ge 0,
 \tag{1}
```
with $`\theta_0`$ $`\mathcal{F}_0`$-measurable and square integrable, and $`V_k`$ $`\mathcal{F}_{k+1}`$-measurable. Norms of matrices are operator norms.

<div id="ass:step" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1.1** (Step sizes). $`\beta_k > 0`$ is deterministic, $`\beta_k \to 0`$, and $`\beta_{k+1}^{-1} - \beta_k^{-1} \to \bar\beta`$ for some $`\bar\beta \in [0,\infty)`$.

</div>

For example $`\beta_k = \alpha/(k + k_0)`$ gives $`\bar\beta = 1/\alpha`$, and $`\beta_k = \alpha/(k+k_0)^\rho`$ with $`\rho < 1`$ gives $`\bar\beta = 0`$. In both cases $`\sum_k\beta_k = \infty`$; this holds in general under Assumption [1.1](#ass:step), since $`\beta_k^{-1} \le \beta_0^{-1} + k(\bar\beta + 1)`$ for large $`k`$.

<div id="ass:stable" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1.2** (Stability). Every eigenvalue of $`A_{\bar\beta} := A - \tfrac{\bar\beta}{2}I`$ has positive real part.

</div>

<div id="ass:noise" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1.3** (Noise). The noise decomposes as
``` math
V_k = M_{k+1} + h_k - h_{k+1},
```
where $`h_k`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_k`$-measurable with $`\sup_k\mathbb{E}\|h_k\|^2 < \infty`$, $`M_{k+1}`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_{k+1}`$-measurable with $`\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = 0`$ and $`\sup_k\mathbb{E}\|M_{k+1}\|^2 < \infty`$, and
``` math
\Gamma_k := \mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M_{k+1}^\top] \longrightarrow \Gamma .
```

</div>

Section [3](#sec:verify) shows that Assumption [1.3](#ass:noise) holds for noise generated by a finite Markov chain and by eligibility traces, with $`\Gamma`$ the long-run covariance $`\sum_{l\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb{E}[V_0V_l^\top]`$.

## 2 Main result

<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 2.1**. Under Assumptions [1.1](#ass:step)–[1.3](#ass:noise),
``` math
\frac{1}{\beta_k}\,\mathbb{E}[\theta_k\theta_k^\top] \longrightarrow \Sigma,
```
where $`\Sigma`$ is the unique solution of the Lyapunov equation
``` math
  A_{\bar\beta}\Sigma + \Sigma A_{\bar\beta}^\top = \Gamma .
 \tag{2}
```

</div>

The proof occupies the rest of this section. Throughout, $`C`$ denotes a constant that does not depend on $`k`$ and may change from line to line.

### 2.1 Step sizes

<div id="lem:step" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2.2**. *Under Assumption [1.1](#ass:step), write $`\beta_k/\beta_{k+1} = 1 + \beta_k(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)`$. Then $`\epsilon_k \to 0`$, $`\beta_{k+1}/\beta_k \to 1`$, and $`|\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k| \le C\beta_k^2`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The identity $`\beta_k/\beta_{k+1} = 1 + \beta_k(\beta_{k+1}^{-1} - \beta_k^{-1})`$ shows $`\epsilon_k = (\beta_{k+1}^{-1} - \beta_k^{-1}) - \bar\beta \to 0`$, and then $`\beta_k/\beta_{k+1}\to1`$ because $`\beta_k \to 0`$. Finally $`|\beta_k - \beta_{k+1}| = \beta_k\beta_{k+1}|\beta_{k+1}^{-1} - \beta_k^{-1}|`$, where the last factor is bounded and $`\beta_{k+1} \le C\beta_k`$. ◻

</div>

### 2.2 Removing the telescoping part of the noise

<div id="lem:cov" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2.3**. Let $`\theta'_k = \theta_k + \beta_kh_k`$. Then
``` math
\theta'_{k+1} = (I - \beta_kA)\theta'_k + \beta_kM_{k+1} + \varepsilon_{k+1},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_{k+1} = \beta_k^2Ah_k + (\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k)h_{k+1},
```
and $`\mathbb{E}\|\varepsilon_{k+1}\|^2 \le C\beta_k^4`$.

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Substituting the decomposition of $`V_k`$ in [(1)](#eq:iter),
``` math
\theta'_{k+1} = \theta_{k+1} + \beta_{k+1}h_{k+1}
  = \theta_k - \beta_kA\theta_k + \beta_kM_{k+1} + \beta_kh_k + (\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k)h_{k+1}.
```
Now $`\theta_k + \beta_kh_k = \theta'_k`$ and $`-\beta_kA\theta_k = -\beta_kA\theta'_k + \beta_k^2Ah_k`$. The bound follows from Lemma [2.2](#lem:step) and $`\sup_k\mathbb{E}\|h_k\|^2 < \infty`$. ◻

</div>

### 2.3 The second-moment recursion

<div id="lem:rec" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2.4**. Let $`P_k = \mathbb{E}[\theta'_k\theta'^\top_k]`$. Then
``` math
P_{k+1} = (I - \beta_kA)P_k(I - \beta_kA)^\top + \beta_k^2\Gamma_k + R_k,
  \qquad
  \|R_k\| \le C\beta_k^2\big(\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k} + \beta_k\big).
```

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Write $`\theta'_{k+1} = a + b + c`$ with $`a = (I - \beta_kA)\theta'_k`$, $`b = \beta_kM_{k+1}`$, $`c = \varepsilon_{k+1}`$. Since $`a`$ is $`\mathcal{F}_k`$-measurable and $`\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = 0`$, the tower property gives $`\mathbb{E}[ab^\top] = 0`$. Hence $`P_{k+1} = \mathbb{E}[aa^\top] + \mathbb{E}[bb^\top] + R_k`$ with $`R_k = \mathbb{E}[ac^\top + ca^\top + bc^\top + cb^\top + cc^\top]`$, and $`\mathbb{E}[bb^\top] = \beta_k^2\Gamma_k`$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $`\|\mathbb{E}[ac^\top]\| \le \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|a\|^2\,\mathbb{E}\|c\|^2} \le C\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k}\,\beta_k^2`$, $`\|\mathbb{E}[bc^\top]\| \le C\beta_k\cdot\beta_k^2`$ and $`\|\mathbb{E}[cc^\top]\| \le C\beta_k^4`$, using Lemma [2.3](#lem:cov). ◻

</div>

### 2.4 An a priori bound

<div id="lem:B" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2.5** (Lyapunov matrix). *There is $`B \succ 0`$ with $`A_{\bar\beta}^\top B + BA_{\bar\beta} = I`$, i.e. $`A^\top B + BA = I + \bar\beta B`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* $`-A_{\bar\beta}`$ is Hurwitz, so $`B = \int_0^\infty e^{-A_{\bar\beta}^\top t}e^{-A_{\bar\beta}t}\,dt`$ converges; it is positive definite, and differentiating $`e^{-A_{\bar\beta}^\top t}e^{-A_{\bar\beta}t}`$ and integrating gives the equation. ◻

</div>

<div id="lem:apriori" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2.6**. *$`\sup_k \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(P_k)/\beta_k < \infty`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Let $`x_k = \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(BP_k)`$, so that $`\lambda_{\min}(B)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k \le x_k \le \lambda_{\max}(B)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k`$. Using Lemma [2.5](#lem:B),
``` math
\begin{align*}
  \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\!\big(B(I - \beta A)P(I - \beta A)^\top\big)
  &= \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\!\big(P\,[B - \beta(A^\top B + BA) + \beta^2A^\top BA]\big) \\
  &= x - \beta\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P - \beta\bar\beta x + \beta^2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(PA^\top BA).
\end{align*}
```
With $`a = 1/\lambda_{\max}(B) > 0`$ we have $`\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P \ge ax`$, so by Lemma [2.4](#lem:rec)
``` math
x_{k+1} \le \big(1 - \beta_k(\bar\beta + a) + C\beta_k^2\big)x_k + C\beta_k^2\big(1 + \sqrt{x_k}\big).
```
Let $`y_k = x_k/\beta_k`$ and use $`\beta_k/\beta_{k+1} = 1 + \beta_k(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)`$ (Lemma [2.2](#lem:step)) and $`\beta_k\sqrt{x_k} = \beta_k\sqrt{\beta_ky_k} \le \beta_k(1 + \beta_ky_k)`$:
``` math
\begin{align*}
  y_{k+1} &\le \big(1 + \beta_k(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)\big)\big(1 - \beta_k(\bar\beta + a) + C\beta_k^2\big)y_k
    + C\beta_k \\
  &= \big(1 - \beta_k(a - \epsilon_k + O(\beta_k))\big)y_k + C\beta_k .
\end{align*}
```
Choose $`k_1`$ such that for $`k \ge k_1`$ the coefficient is at most $`1 - a\beta_k/2`$ and $`a\beta_k/2 \le 1`$. Then $`Y = \max(y_{k_1}, 2C/a)`$ satisfies: $`y_k \le Y`$ implies $`y_{k+1} \le (1 - a\beta_k/2)Y + C\beta_k \le Y`$. By induction $`y_k \le Y`$ for $`k \ge k_1`$. ◻

</div>

### 2.5 A deterministic lemma

<div id="lem:pert" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2.7**. *Let $`\mathcal{L}`$ be a linear map on a finite-dimensional real vector space whose eigenvalues all have positive real part. Let $`\beta_k \to 0`$ with $`\sum_k\beta_k = \infty`$, let $`\delta_k \to 0`$, and let $`e_{k+1} = (I - \beta_k\mathcal{L})e_k + \beta_k\delta_k`$. Then $`e_k \to 0`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* As in Lemma [2.5](#lem:B) there is an inner product $`\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_*`$ with $`\langle\mathcal{L}v, v\rangle_* \ge a\|v\|_*^2`$ for some $`a > 0`$. Then $`\|(I - \beta\mathcal{L})v\|_*^2 = \|v\|_*^2 - 2\beta\langle\mathcal{L}v, v\rangle_* + \beta^2\|\mathcal{L}v\|_*^2
\le (1 - 2a\beta + C\beta^2)\|v\|_*^2`$, so $`\|I - \beta_k\mathcal{L}\|_* \le 1 - a\beta_k`$ for large $`k`$, and
``` math
\|e_{k+1}\|_* \le (1 - a\beta_k)\|e_k\|_* + \beta_k\|\delta_k\|_* .
```
Fix $`\eta > 0`$ and $`k_2`$ with $`\|\delta_k\|_* \le a\eta`$ for $`k \ge k_2`$. Then $`u_k = \|e_k\|_* - \eta`$ satisfies $`u_{k+1} \le (1 - a\beta_k)u_k`$ for $`k\ge k_2`$. If $`u_k \le 0`$ for some $`k \ge k_2`$ it stays so; otherwise $`u_k \le u_{k_2}\prod_{j=k_2}^{k-1}(1 - a\beta_j) \to 0`$ because $`\sum\beta_j = \infty`$. Hence $`\limsup\|e_k\|_* \le \eta`$ for every $`\eta > 0`$. ◻

</div>

### 2.6 Proof of Theorem [2.1](#thm:main)

Let $`S_k = P_k/\beta_k`$ and $`\mathcal A S = AS + SA^\top`$. By Lemma [2.4](#lem:rec),
``` math
\frac{P_{k+1}}{\beta_k} = S_k - \beta_k\mathcal AS_k + \beta_k^2AS_kA^\top
    + \beta_k\Gamma_k + \beta_k\frac{R_k}{\beta_k^2}.
```
Multiplying by $`\beta_k/\beta_{k+1} = 1 + \beta_k(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)`$,
``` math
S_{k+1} = S_k - \beta_k\big(\mathcal AS_k - \bar\beta S_k - \Gamma\big) + \beta_k\delta_k,
```
where
``` math
\delta_k = \epsilon_kS_k + (\Gamma_k - \Gamma) + \beta_kAS_kA^\top + \frac{R_k}{\beta_k^2}
    + \beta_k(\bar\beta + \epsilon_k)\Big(-\mathcal AS_k + \beta_kAS_kA^\top + \Gamma_k + \frac{R_k}{\beta_k^2}\Big).
```
By Lemma [2.6](#lem:apriori), $`S_k`$ is bounded and $`\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k = O(\beta_k)`$, so $`\|R_k\|/\beta_k^2 \le C(\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k} + \beta_k) \to 0`$; with $`\epsilon_k \to 0`$ and $`\Gamma_k \to \Gamma`$, every term of $`\delta_k`$ tends to zero.

Define $`\mathcal{L}S = \mathcal AS - \bar\beta S = A_{\bar\beta}S + SA_{\bar\beta}^\top`$ on symmetric matrices. Its eigenvalues are the sums $`\lambda_i + \lambda_j`$ of eigenvalues of $`A_{\bar\beta}`$, which have positive real part; in particular $`\mathcal{L}`$ is invertible and [(2)](#eq:lyap) has a unique solution $`\Sigma`$. With $`e_k = S_k - \Sigma`$,
``` math
e_{k+1} = (I - \beta_k\mathcal{L})e_k + \beta_k\delta_k ,
```
so $`e_k \to 0`$ by Lemma [2.7](#lem:pert) (recall $`\sum\beta_k = \infty`$).

Finally, $`\theta_k = \theta'_k - \beta_kh_k`$, so
``` math
\big\|\mathbb{E}[\theta_k\theta_k^\top] - P_k\big\| \le 2\beta_k\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|h_k\|^2\,\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}P_k} + \beta_k^2\mathbb{E}\|h_k\|^2
  = O(\beta_k^{3/2}),
```
and therefore $`\beta_k^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\theta_k\theta_k^\top] - S_k \to 0`$.  ◻

## 3 Verifying the noise assumption

### 3.1 Noise generated by a finite Markov chain

Let $`(Y_k)`$ be a Markov chain on a finite set $`\mathcal{Y}`$ with irreducible aperiodic transition matrix $`Q`$ and stationary law $`\nu`$, $`\mathcal{F}_k = \sigma(\theta_0, Y_0,\dots,Y_k)`$, and $`V_k = v(Y_k, Y_{k+1})`$ with $`\sum_{y,y'}\nu(y)Q(y,y')v(y,y') = 0`$. Let $`\bar v(y) = \sum_{y'}Q(y,y')v(y,y')`$, let $`Z = (I - Q + \mathbf 1\nu^\top)^{-1}`$ be the fundamental matrix, and $`\hat h = Z\bar v`$ (applied componentwise).

<div id="lem:poisson" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3.1** (Poisson equation). *$`\hat h - Q\hat h = \bar v`$, and with $`M_{k+1} = v(Y_k,Y_{k+1}) - \bar v(Y_k) + \hat h(Y_{k+1}) - (Q\hat h)(Y_k)`$ and $`h_k = \hat h(Y_k)`$ we have $`V_k = M_{k+1} + h_k - h_{k+1}`$ and $`\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = 0`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* $`\nu^\top\bar v = 0`$ by assumption, so $`\mathbf 1\nu^\top\bar v = 0`$ and $`(I - Q)Z\bar v = (I - Q + \mathbf 1\nu^\top)Z\bar v = \bar v`$. The decomposition follows from $`\bar v = \hat h - Q\hat h`$, and $`\mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_k] = \bar v(Y_k) - \bar v(Y_k) +
(Q\hat h)(Y_k) - (Q\hat h)(Y_k) = 0`$ by the Markov property. ◻

</div>

<div id="lem:lrc" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3.2** (Long-run covariance). Let $`m(y) = \mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M_{k+1}^\top\mid Y_k = y]`$ (which does not depend on $`k`$) and $`\Gamma = \sum_y\nu(y)m(y)`$. Then $`\Gamma_k \to \Gamma`$ geometrically fast for every initial law, and
``` math
\Gamma = \sum_{l\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb{E}_\nu[V_0V_l^\top],
```
the series converging absolutely.

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* $`\Gamma_k = \sum_y\mathbb P(Y_k = y)m(y)`$ and $`\mathbb P(Y_k = \cdot) \to \nu`$ geometrically. For the identity, start the chain from $`\nu`$ and let $`T_n = \sum_{k<n}V_k
= \sum_{k<n}M_{k+1} + \hat h(Y_0) - \hat h(Y_n)`$. The martingale differences are orthogonal, so $`\frac1n\mathbb{E}[T_nT_n^\top] \to \Gamma`$, the boundary terms contributing $`O(n^{-1/2})`$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. On the other hand $`\frac1n\mathbb{E}[T_nT_n^\top] = \sum_{|l|<n}(1 - |l|/n)\mathbb{E}_\nu[V_0V_l^\top]`$, and for $`l \ge 1`$, $`\mathbb{E}_\nu[V_0V_l^\top] = \mathbb{E}_\nu[V_0\,(Q^{l-1}\bar v)(Y_1)^\top]`$ with $`\|Q^{l-1}\bar v\|_\infty \le C\rho^{l-1}`$ for some $`\rho < 1`$, since $`\nu^\top\bar v = 0`$. So the series converges absolutely, and the Cesàro means converge to its sum. ◻

</div>

<div id="cor:finite" class="corollary">

**Corollary 3.3**. *For noise generated by a finite Markov chain, Assumption [1.3](#ass:noise) holds and Theorem [2.1](#thm:main) applies with $`\Gamma`$ the long-run covariance of the noise.*

</div>

### 3.2 Noise generated by eligibility traces

In TD($`\lambda`$) and in actor-only and actor-critic methods the noise depends on a trace, $`f_k = F(Y_k,Y_{k+1})z_k + e(Y_k,Y_{k+1})`$ with $`z_k = c(Y_k)z_{k-1} + \phi(Y_k)`$, where the spectral radius of $`Q\operatorname{diag}(c)`$ is less than one. The pair $`(Y_k, z_k)`$ is a Markov chain on an infinite state space, but its Poisson equation has an explicit affine solution. Let $`U`$ and $`h`$ be as in the closed form for $`\Gamma`$ (TD($`\lambda`$) chapter, Theorem 4.4, and `experiments/trace_cov.py`): $`U = \bar F + Q\operatorname{diag}(c)U`$, $`w = \bar e + Q[U\phi]`$, $`h = Zw`$.

<div id="lem:trace-poisson" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3.4**. $`H(y,z) = U(y)z + h(y)`$ solves the Poisson equation of the chain $`(Y_k, z_k)`$:
``` math
H(y,z) - \mathbb{E}\big[H(Y_1, z_1)\mid Y_0 = y, z_0 = z\big] = \bar F(y)z + \bar e(y)
    = \mathbb{E}[f_0\mid Y_0 = y, z_0 = z].
```

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Since $`z_1 = c(Y_1)z + \phi(Y_1)`$, the conditional expectation equals $`\big(Q\operatorname{diag}(c)U\big)(y)\,z + \big(Q[U\phi]\big)(y) + (Qh)(y)`$. Subtracting from $`H(y,z)`$ and using $`U - Q\operatorname{diag}(c)U = \bar F`$ and $`h - Qh = w - \mathbf 1\nu^\top w = w`$ (because $`\nu^\top w = \mathbb{E}f_0 = 0`$) leaves $`\bar F(y)z + w(y) - (Q[U\phi])(y) = \bar F(y)z + \bar e(y)`$. ◻

</div>

<div id="cor:trace" class="corollary">

**Corollary 3.5**. *With $`h_k = H(Y_k,z_k)`$ and $`M_{k+1} = f_k - \mathbb{E}[f_k\mid\mathcal{F}_k] + H(Y_{k+1},z_{k+1}) -
\mathbb{E}[H(Y_{k+1},z_{k+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_k]`$, trace-driven noise satisfies Assumption [1.3](#ass:noise) whenever the traces have bounded second moments (for instance $`\sup_yc(y) < 1`$, or traces reset at restarts), and $`\Gamma = \mathbb{E}[M_{k+1}M_{k+1}^\top]`$ under stationarity equals the closed-form long-run covariance of the TD($`\lambda`$) chapter.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The decomposition $`f_k = M_{k+1} + h_k - h_{k+1}`$ and the martingale property follow from Lemma [3.4](#lem:trace-poisson) exactly as in Lemma [3.1](#lem:poisson). The identification of $`\Gamma`$ follows as in Lemma [3.2](#lem:lrc), using that the trace forgets its initial value geometrically. ◻

</div>

<div class="remark">

**Remark 3.6** (Numerical checks). (i) For a random 7-state chain, $`\mathbb{E}_\nu[MM^\top]`$ computed from Lemma [3.1](#lem:poisson) equals the long-run covariance to relative error $`3\times10^{-16}`$; for trace-driven noise, the corresponding quantity from Lemma [3.4](#lem:trace-poisson) equals the closed form to $`7\times10^{-16}`$. (ii) For a $`2\times2`$ iteration with Markov noise and $`\beta_k = 2/(k + 50)`$, a simulation with $`20{,}000`$ runs gives $`\beta_k^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\theta_k\theta_k^\top] \approx \begin{pmatrix}0.482 & -0.075\\ -0.075 & 0.518\end{pmatrix}`$ at $`k = 40{,}000`$, against $`\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}0.479 & -0.070\\ -0.070 & 0.523\end{pmatrix}`$ from [(2)](#eq:lyap).

</div>

## 4 What remains

- *Two time scales.* The same reduction to martingale differences applies to each component; the second-moment analysis then follows Konda (2002, Thm. 7.6), with the cross covariance $`\Gamma_{12}`$ of the two martingale parts. The goal is the analogue of Thm. 7.8 for Markov noise: the slow component has the covariance of the single-time-scale iteration with noise $`V - A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}W`$.

- *Multiplicative noise.* In TD and actor-critic the matrix $`A`$ is itself random, $`A(Y_k,z_k)`$. Its fluctuation $`A(Y_k,z_k) - \bar A`$ has a Poisson decomposition too, and contributes terms of order $`\beta_k\,\mathbb{E}\|\theta_k\|^2 = O(\beta_k^2)`$ to the recursion, once an a priori bound like Lemma [2.6](#lem:apriori) is established.

- *Nonlinear algorithms.* The transfer from the linearized iteration to the actual actor-critic algorithm is left as an explicit hypothesis.
